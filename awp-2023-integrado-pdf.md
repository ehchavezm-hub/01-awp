# Features of an EWP
1. EWPs represent a scope of work for a single discipline within a given Construction Work Area. They exist on a 1:1 basis with CWPs.
2. EWPs will be delivered in the form of a document which lists all relevant Engineering information, including scope of work, drawings, specifications, material supply and model shots.
3. EWPs are area-based, not system based. They include elements from multiple systems, and will typically not include systems in their entirety.
4. The scope elements within EWPs do not cross a CWA boundary, except in exceptional circumstances, such as the installation of long cables that cannot be broken down.

# Features of a CWP
1. CWPs represent a scope of work for a single discipline within a given Construction Work Area, to facilitate construction planning and prioritization of construction activities.
2. CWPs will be delivered in the form of a document which lists all relevant construction information, including scope of work, technical documents, safety, quality and execution aspects.
3. CWPs are area-based, not system based. They include elements from multiple systems, and will typically not include systems in their entirety.
4. The scope elements within CWPs do not cross a CWA boundary, except in exceptional circumstances, such as the installation of long cables that cannot be broken down.

# CWP Content - Recommendations
1. The EWP will form the technical basis of the CWP,
## but the EWP will not be given to the contractor. So make sure all relevant information is in the CWP.
2. Be careful with language that could be interpreted as an instruction.
3. Include clear definition of the limit of the scope, and what work will be done by others.
4. Avoid the use of boilerplate wording. If it is said somewhere else, don’t repeat it.
5. Don’t include contractual language in a CWP. It is not the right place for it.
6. For supplied items, such as vendor equipment, identify scope remaining to be done at site.

# Step 1 - Review the CWP

2. Meet with the Construction Manager or Superintendent for that trade.
3. Review the scope contained within the CWP.
4. Agree the preferred installation sequence for the scope of the CWP, which willprovide you with the priority sequence for creating the IWPs.
5. Agree any firm scope breaks. (e.g. The installation of a large piece ofequipment should be a standalone IWP).
# Step 2 - Break the CWP into IWPs
1. Using the agreed priority sequence, break the CWP scope into IWP-sizedpackages.
## a. Remember to include any agreed scope breaks
## b. IWPs should be enough work for one crew for one shift - Typically 500 to 1000
## hours. The work should be able to be completed without stopping.
## c. Make sure that the breaks in IWPs are logical. (e.g. Don’t stop an IWP halfway
## through a piping isometric or cable pull).
2. Add the IWPs to the IWP Release Plan, and work with the project schedulerto agree the planned dates for execution of the work for each IWP.
# Step 3 - Create drawing list and check availability

2. Include all the drawings that a crew will need, but nothing more.
3. Include any standard drawings or vendor information. (Don’t include the wholepackage - just what is needed for the IWP).
4. If the site layout is complex, include a copy of the plot plan showing the work area.
5. Verify the drawing list against the master document list to check that all documentsare available IFC. If any are missing, issue an RFI to request the missingdocuments.

# How to Calculate Backlog
## Backlog can be expressed in two ways:
## Total Hours Available - This is just the sum of the hours (either by discipline or for all disciplines) of all constraint-free IWPs.
## Or..
## Total Days - The total hours available is divided by the current site manpower to express backlog in terms of how many days of work is available.
### *For example, if you have 50 people on site, working 10 hours per day, they should earn 500 manhours per day.*
### *If you have 5,000 hours in available IWPs, your backlog is 10 days, which is the time it would take those 50 people to earn the 5,000 hours.*
# Uses for Backlog
